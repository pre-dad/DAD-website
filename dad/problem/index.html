The problem

People didn't stop wanting to help. They stopped believing it worked.

$617.2 billion.

Given by Americans last year. Up 5%.

3.6% fewer donors.

Fourth year in a row.

More money. Fewer people. That's not a generosity problem. It's a trust problem.

In New York alone!

  1. $85 to raise every $100.

    Two cancer charities in New York spent 85% of donations raising more donations.

  2. 104 campaigns lost money.

    In one year in New York, they cost more to run than they raised. More than $20 million, gone.

  1. $395 million to fundraising companies.

    One state. One year. They kept an average of 25 cents of every dollar.

  2. 86 cents of every dollar.

    One national children's charity paid $109.8 million of the $127.8 million it raised to professional solicitors.

  1. $187 million.

    Four cancer charities, sued by the FTC and all fifty states. Donations meant for patients paid for cars, cruises and tuition.

  2. 9 cents.

    At one charity, that's how much of each dollar of cash spending reached the programs.

Every one of them looked legitimate.

The government's own auditors count roughly $3 trillion in improper payments since 2003. Every administration. Both parties. The machinery is the problem, not the people running it.

Nobody here is short of good intent. They're running machinery built before the internet. And the machinery is where the money goes.

01 · Money moves slowly.

Layers of approval, reconciliation and reporting sit between your gift and the work.

02 · Checking is expensive.

Confirming help arrived costs more than the help. So most organizations sample it, or skip it.

03 · Overhead eats the dollar.

Structures built to raise money consume the money they raise.

04 · Impact is reported, not shown.

An annual PDF, written by the people being judged.

05 · Relief ends before stability starts.

Emergency help fixes the moment and leaves the conditions that caused it.

Generosity isn't broken. Delivery is.

Five years ago this couldn't be built. Now it can.

  1. AI.

    Checking, matching and reporting at almost no cost. The three jobs that made real transparency unaffordable.

  2. Creators.

    Reach that used to cost hundreds of millions is now earned. Proof travels further than ads ever did.

  3. Real-time money.

    Funds that settle in minutes, with a record of where they went, cheap enough to make a dollar a day work.

  4. Digital verification.

    Need, delivery and outcome confirmed on a phone. No office. No paper trail.

  5. The disillusioned.

    Millions of people who still want to give, still can, and are looking for somewhere to believe in.

The world changed. Giving didn't. Until now.
